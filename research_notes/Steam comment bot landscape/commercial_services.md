# Commercial / SaaS Steam Engagement Services

Research date: 2026-11-18. Primary tool available was WebSearch; WebFetch to provider domains (boosting-service.com, imbaboost.com, mediamister.com, steamcommunity.com, playerauctions.com, trustpilot.com) was blocked by this session's network policy, so all pricing and feature claims are taken from WebSearch result snippets rather than fetched provider pages. Where a fact only lives in an aggregator excerpt, that is noted.

## Who are the major commercial providers of Steam profile engagement?

### Takeaway
The Steam engagement market is a long tail of small, often short-lived storefronts rather than a few dominant SaaS brands. The recurring, searchable names are boosting-service.com, boosting-service.cloud, imbaboost.com, freehourboost.com, Boost Comments LLC (sold via a Steam group), Jeffrey's / Evox / Daniel / SALVA / SEAL / Boostinggmbh "Boosting Service" style Steam groups, plus listings on PlayerAuctions and gigs on Fiverr. General-purpose social-media SMM providers (Media Mister, SocialBoss, buyviews.co, SMM panels like JustAnotherPanel and Peakerr) show up in roundups but public evidence that they carry Steam SKUs is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Trustpilot hosts review pages for the following Steam-focused boosting storefronts: `boosting-service.com`, `boosting-service.cloud`, `imbaboost.com`, `freehourboost.com`, and `smmsem.com`, which indicates these are the longest-running branded sellers — [Boosting Service cloud Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.cloud); [Boosting-service.com Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.com); [imbaboost.com Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/imbaboost.com); [freehourboost.com Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/freehourboost.com); [smmsem.com Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/smmsem.com).
- A Steam-group storefront branded "Boost Comments LLC" sells comment packages in EUR and runs monthly subscriptions — [Boost Comments LLC Steam group](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/boostcommentsllc).
- Other Steam-group-based sellers that surface in searches: JeffreysBoostingService, evoxboostingservice, BSI101, Boostinggmbh, DanielBoostingService, SALVABOOST, SEAL_BOOSTING, memeboostingcsgo, BOOSTING_SERVICES, iboosting — [Jeffreys Boosting Service](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/JeffreysBoostingService); [EvoxBoostingService](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/evoxboostingservice/comments); [BSI101](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/BSI101); [Boostinggmbh](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/Boostinggmbh/comments); [Daniel Boosting Service](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/DanielBoostingService/announcements); [SALVABOOST](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/SALVABOOST/comments); [SEAL_BOOSTING](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/SEAL_BOOSTING/comments); [BOOSTING_SERVICES](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/BOOSTING_SERVICES); [iboosting](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/iboosting).

### Inferences
- The branded Steam-specific storefronts cluster around a handful of "boosting-service"-style domains plus Steam community groups, suggesting the market is dominated by resellers plugged into a few shared supply pools rather than independent SaaS vendors.
- Generalist SMM/SMM-panel operators (Media Mister, SocialBoss, Peakerr, JustAnotherPanel) are marketed heavily for Instagram/TikTok/YouTube; Steam does not show up in their public service lists based on the roundups and sourceforge/slashdot listings that were returned.

### Gaps
- No direct fetches of provider pricing pages were possible, so storefront-level service lists for mediamister.com, socialboss.org, buyviews.co, JustAnotherPanel and Peakerr could not be verified. Those domains need to be allowlisted to enumerate SKUs.
- No sourced data on steamfollowers.com — search returned only unrelated hits for "SteemFollowers" (a Steem blockchain tool). A targeted fetch is needed.

## What do they sell and at what price per 100 / 1000?

### Takeaway
Across the branded sellers, the common SKUs are Steam profile comments, group comments, screenshot/artwork likes-or-favorites, profile followers, and Steam Points. Advertised prices cluster around US$1 per 1,000 profile comments on the low (likely-botted) end, €15–€40 per 1,000 at the branded-reseller end, and specialist Workshop-vote or screenshot-targeted packages at higher per-unit prices. Workshop votes and wishlist adds exist but mostly through freelancer gigs, not automated SKUs.

### Cited Findings
- Boost Comments LLC (Steam group storefront): 60 comments for €5 (snippet summary; see group page), 250 comments for €15, 1,000 for €40, 5,000 for €80, plus monthly subscriptions of €30–€60 for 30–90 comments/day — [Boost Comments LLC Steam group](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/boostcommentsllc) (prices surfaced via WebSearch snippet summarizing the group's package listings).
- Epic NPC branded reseller: "1K = $1" Steam profile comments, with packages up to 20,000 comments for $19.90 — [Epic NPC listing](https://www.epicnpc.com/threads/%F0%9F%8E%AF-steam-profile-comments-1k-1-no-vac.2201584/).
- PlayerAuctions: 300 Steam comments listed at $5.50, 1,000 at $6.50 (search excerpt), 3,000 likes-or-favorites at a higher tier — [PlayerAuctions 300 comments](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/294565000p!300-steam-comments--no-vac-/); [PlayerAuctions 1,000 comments](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/296457074p!1000-steam-comments--no-vac-/); [PlayerAuctions 3,000 likes/favorites](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/291138667p!steam-profile--3000-likes-or-favorites--profiles--/).
- Paid positive Steam reviews were historically sold on Fiverr for about $5 per review — [PCGamesN, 2016](https://www.pcgamesn.com/its-about-ethics-in-user-reviews-how-to-get-a-positive-steam-critique-for-$5).
- Telegram SMM panels (as a yardstick for SMM-panel pricing scale) list members at ~$0.18–$0.20 per 1,000, which indicates the floor for commoditized panel traffic — [SMM panel pricing overview](https://blog.crescitaly.com/smm-panel-pricing-what-smart-marketers-need-to-know-in-2026/).
- Fiverr Steam wishlist gigs are priced per-gig rather than per-unit; pricing is custom — [Fiverr Steam wishlist gig](https://fiverr.com/emmatolu_/do-steam-game-promotion-to-boost-steam-wishlist); [Fiverr Steam wishlist/traffic](https://fiverr.com/hellosteampower/do-steam-game-promotion-steam-wishlist-and-traffic).
- PlayerAuctions' listings include a "25 likes or favorites" entry, suggesting low-floor micro SKUs exist for showcase content — [PlayerAuctions 25 likes/favorites](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/291138635p!steam-profile--25-likes-or-favorites--profiles--wo/).

### Inferences
- Branded Steam resellers price profile comments roughly 20–40× above the per-unit SMM-panel wholesale floor, which is consistent with a two-tier supply chain: a small number of operators run bot farms and the storefronts resell at markup.
- The Epic NPC $1-per-1K rate is a plausible wholesale indicator; the €40-per-1K Boost Comments LLC rate is a plausible branded retail indicator. The retail markup implies either higher-quality (aged/L2+) accounts, lower churn, or pure positioning — the sources don't distinguish.
- Workshop-upvote and wishlist-add SKUs appear almost exclusively as Fiverr-style custom gigs, not as parameterized "per 100" SKUs, which suggests delivery is manual rather than automated for those targets.

### Gaps
- Per-SKU prices for screenshot comments, artwork comments, group comments, workshop comments, workshop votes, follows, group joins, and wishlist adds on the branded storefronts (boosting-service.cloud, imbaboost.com, freehourboost.com) could not be confirmed from indexed snippets. Direct provider fetches or archive.org snapshots are needed.
- No current (2026) MediaMister/SocialBoss Steam SKU list or price was surfaced.

## Delivery speed and drip-feeding

### Takeaway
Evidence is partial: some storefronts advertise "automated through Discord bots" / "instant" delivery, Media Mister-style generalists advertise "gradual delivery," and complaints frequently cite multi-day waits or non-delivery. No cited Steam-specific storefront explicitly markets a drip-feed scheduler in the way Instagram SMM panels do.

### Cited Findings
- Boosting-service.cloud describes its service as automated through Discord bots, implying near-instant delivery after order — [Boosting-service.cloud Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.cloud) (via WebSearch excerpt).
- Trustpilot complaint: a buyer paid for 200 comments on a boosting-service domain and received nothing after five days — [Boosting-service.com Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.com).
- Trustpilot complaint: a buyer paid 19 USDT for 1,000 comments on boosting-service.cloud and received nothing — [Boosting-service.cloud Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.cloud).
- Positive review of imbaboost.com: comments arrived with "organic-looking accounts" — [imbaboost.com Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/imbaboost.com).
- Media Mister (generalist) markets "gradual delivery" with country targeting and no-password ordering — [Media Mister 2025 review](https://redditchstandard.co.uk/?p=122965).
- PlayerAuctions listings generally note the target content must be public and that no password is required — [PlayerAuctions 3,000 likes/favorites listing](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/291138667p!steam-profile--3000-likes-or-favorites--profiles--/).

### Inferences
- The Discord-bot automation pattern suggests delivery is near-instant when fulfilled, but queue backlogs and silent failures are common enough to generate repeated non-delivery complaints.
- "Drip-feed" as a scheduling feature is not visibly a Steam-storefront differentiator the way it is on Instagram SMM panels; branded Steam resellers appear to ship in bursts.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence of explicit per-hour/per-day drip settings on a Steam storefront. Confirming this needs a fetch of imbaboost / boosting-service.cloud / freehourboost pricing pages.

## API, dashboards, desktop clients

### Takeaway
Every sourced Steam storefront appears to run an order form + Discord-bot pipeline rather than a self-serve dashboard or API. SMM-panel software (JustAnotherPanel, Peakerr, Perfectpanel installations) exposes an HTTP API by default, but no cited source confirms any of them carry Steam services. No commercial desktop client for Steam engagement surfaces in the research; the only desktop/self-hosted tooling is open-source bots on GitHub, notably `3urobeat/steam-comment-service-bot`.

### Cited Findings
- Boosting-service.cloud workflow is described as Discord-bot automated — [Boosting-service.cloud Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.cloud).
- SMM-panel API pattern: submit service ID + target link + quantity, receive order ID and status — [SMM reseller panel explainer](https://geekchamp.com/what-is-an-smm-panel-and-how-does-it-work/); [Medium SMM reseller panel](https://medium.com/smmeshop/what-are-the-features-and-benefits-of-smm-reseller-panels-b20d4da084d7).
- JustAnotherPanel markets itself on low prices and large service catalog with "API-oriented approach"; SourceForge user score is 2.0/5 — [SourceForge JustAnotherPanel listing](https://www.sourceforge.net/software/product/JustAnotherPanel).
- Peakerr's Nov-2025 press piece lists only TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Telegram as covered platforms; Steam is not named — [Peakerr press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/31827354/peakerr-launches-one-of-the-most-accessible-and-affordable-smm-panels-worldwide).
- Open-source alternative: `3urobeat/steam-comment-service-bot` is a self-hosted Node.js tool advertising "mass coordinated comments/likes/favs/follows" and a plugin ecosystem including a Discord plugin and a REST API plugin, with an official Docker image — [GitHub project](https://github.com/HerrEurobeat/steam-comment-service-bot); [Docker Hub image](https://hub.docker.com/r/3urobeat/steam-comment-service-bot); [REST API plugin project](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fderdeathraven%2Fsteam-comment-bot-rest-api); [plugin wiki](https://github.com/3urobeat/steam-comment-service-bot/blob/beta-testing/docs/wiki/creating_plugins.md).
- Related open-source tools: `3urobeat/steam-mass-comment-bot` (one-to-many), `hschickdevs/steam-comment-bot`, `rep4rep/rep4rep-bot` — [steam-mass-comment-bot](https://www.github.com/3urobeat/steam-mass-comment-bot); [hschickdevs steam-comment-bot](https://redirect.github.com/hschickdevs/steam-comment-bot); [rep4rep-bot](https://github.com/rep4rep/rep4rep-bot).

### Inferences
- The absence of a branded desktop client is the clearest gap in the commercial market: paying buyers interact through a web order form or a Discord bot, and the only control-plane tooling that exists is the open-source bot ecosystem.
- Any competitive desktop product has to match: (a) multi-target queueing (profile / group / screenshot / artwork / workshop item), (b) rate-paced delivery to mimic drip, (c) per-link targeting from a URL paste, (d) a credential vault for many Steam accounts, (e) optional REST API for scripting — all features the `3urobeat` bot already partially covers.

### Gaps
- No direct confirmation that any paid storefront offers an end-customer dashboard or public API. Claims of "API access" from branded Steam resellers would need a fetch of their docs or pricing pages.

## Account supply (botted vs. aged vs. L2+)

### Takeaway
Supply is not publicly disclosed by any sourced provider. Indirect signals (price tiers, complaints about "non-organic" accounts, positive reviews mentioning "organic-looking accounts") suggest a mix of bulk-created low-level bot accounts at the low end and leveled/aged accounts at the branded-reseller end, but no provider states its account source.

### Cited Findings
- Positive imbaboost.com review notes "organic-looking accounts" — [imbaboost.com Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/imbaboost.com).
- Epic NPC branded reseller labels its SKU "No VAC," implying the commenting accounts are not VAC-banned, but gives no level or age data — [Epic NPC listing](https://www.epicnpc.com/threads/%F0%9F%8E%AF-steam-profile-comments-1k-1-no-vac.2201584/).
- PlayerAuctions "No VAC" tag appears across multiple Steam-boosting listings — [PlayerAuctions 1,000 comments](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/296457074p!1000-steam-comments--no-vac-/).
- Article on the dev side notes that developers have been "called out for allegedly purchasing positive Steam reviews," confirming the buyer market for inauthentic engagement exists but doesn't disclose supply — [MCV UK](https://mcvuk.com/devs-called-out-for-allegedly-purchasing-positive-steam-reviews/).

### Inferences
- "No VAC" as the only quality tag implies the dominant selling point is ban-avoidance, not account authenticity, consistent with botted supply.
- The 40× price gap between the $1/1K wholesale tier and the €40/1K branded tier is plausibly the premium for leveled/aged (L2+/L10+) accounts, but the sources do not confirm this.

### Gaps
- No provider publicly discloses whether its comment-source accounts are bulk-created, bought from account marketplaces, aged, or leveled up via trading cards. No cited source states account levels or Steam-community-age distribution.

## Refund, guarantee, and refill policies

### Takeaway
Branded generalist sellers (Media Mister) advertise refund protection and gradual delivery as part of positioning. Steam-specific storefronts have no sourced published refund policy; Trustpilot evidence shows non-delivery is a frequent and often unresolved complaint, suggesting refund enforcement is weak in practice.

### Cited Findings
- Media Mister (generalist) marketed with refund protection and gradual delivery — [Media Mister 2025 review](https://redditchstandard.co.uk/?p=122965).
- SocialBoss: roundup notes "Outdated site, no clear refund or cancellation terms" — [Trusted TikTok roundup](https://pressbanner.com/?p=20549074).
- Boosting-service.com has multiple Trustpilot complaints alleging non-delivery with no refund issued, including "exit scam" accusations going back to 2020 — [Boosting-service.com Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.com).
- Boosting-service.cloud has a non-delivery complaint (19 USDT, 1,000 comments) with a company reply visible but reply text not captured in snippet — [Boosting-service.cloud Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/boosting-service.cloud).

### Inferences
- The strongest refund/refill signal in the market is the generalist SMM playbook (refund protection + replacement/refill after N days). Steam-specific storefronts appear to under-invest in these policies.
- Any product that competes with these services doesn't need a stronger refund story to beat them; a visibly working delivery pipeline already surpasses the median storefront.

### Gaps
- No sourced published refill-period (e.g. "30-day refill guarantee") from any Steam-specific storefront.

Based on cited material, their catalogs emphasize Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Telegram, Facebook. Steam is not a visibly-featured category on any cited top panel, implying Steam traffic is a niche vertical sold mostly through dedicated Steam storefronts, not general SMM resellers.

## Per-link targeting (specific profile, screenshot, workshop item)

### Takeaway
Per-link targeting is the core feature of the market: buyers paste a Steam URL (profile, screenshot, artwork, workshop item, group) and the service delivers to that target. This is confirmed on PlayerAuctions listings and implicit in every branded storefront's workflow.

### Cited Findings
- PlayerAuctions listings require the content to be public and accept per-target URLs; no account password required — [PlayerAuctions 3,000 likes/favorites](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/291138667p!steam-profile--3000-likes-or-favorites--profiles--/); [PlayerAuctions 25 likes/favorites](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/291138635p!steam-profile--25-likes-or-favorites--profiles--wo/).
- PlayerAuctions listings caveat that some games, profiles, Workshop items, and Steam content may not be supported — [PlayerAuctions 3,000 likes/favorites listing](https://www.playerauctions.com/steam-boosting/291138667p!steam-profile--3000-likes-or-favorites--profiles--/).
- SMM-panel API pattern explicitly takes target link as a required input — [SMM panel explainer](https://geekchamp.com/what-is-an-smm-panel-and-how-does-it-work/).
- Open-source `steam-mass-comment-bot` supports sending to profiles, groups, and discussions — [steam-mass-comment-bot](https://www.github.com/3urobeat/steam-mass-comment-bot).

### Inferences
- Any competitive desktop product must accept a URL and resolve it to the right endpoint type (profile / group / screenshot / artwork / workshop item / discussion thread / curator). The open-source `3urobeat` bot already models this; paid services clearly do too.
- The PlayerAuctions "not all content supported" caveat is likely driven by Workshop vote mechanics and private/friends-only showcases, which the supply pool cannot interact with.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence on whether branded storefronts accept Workshop-comment URLs vs only Workshop-vote URLs. Needs provider-page fetch.

## Overall market shape (bonus notes)

### Cited Findings
- Developer-side purchase of positive Steam reviews has been reported in mainstream gaming press, confirming real buyer demand on the publisher side — [TechRaptor devs buying positive reviews](https://techraptor.net/content/report-developers-buying-positive-steam-reviews); [MCV UK](https://mcvuk.com/devs-called-out-for-allegedly-purchasing-positive-steam-reviews/).
- A free peer-exchange alternative exists: `rep4rep.com` runs a comment-exchange Steam group and public API — [rep4rep Steam group](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/rep4repcom); [rep4rep-bot](https://github.com/rep4rep/rep4rep-bot).
- `steampoints.com` and `steamlevelup.com` are adjacent Steam-economy services with large Trustpilot footprints, which shows that Steam-economy adjacent SaaS can scale — [SteamPoints Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/steampoints.com); [SteamLevelUp Trustpilot](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/steamlevelup.com?page=6).

### Inferences
- Two real buyer segments exist: (1) individual Steam users pumping their own profile/showcase vanity metrics, (2) indie developers/publishers buying Workshop votes, wishlist adds, and positive reviews. Current commercial storefronts serve segment 1 well and segment 2 only through Fiverr-style custom gigs.
- The clearest competitive white space for a desktop product is: a polished local client that ties together profile-comment supply + screenshot/artwork likes + follows + group joins + Workshop-vote targeting, with per-link URL paste, rate-pacing, and multi-account management — matching Discord-bot speed with better UX than a Steam-group order form.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) pricing for every named provider could not be confirmed from indexed snippets alone; a verification pass with WebFetch/archive.org on allowlisted domains would tighten the numbers. The specific domains worth fetching: boosting-service.cloud, imbaboost.com, freehourboost.com, mediamister.com/buy-steam-*, socialboss.org, buyviews.co, justanotherpanel.com, peakerr.com, playerauctions.com/steam-boosting, steamcommunity.com group pages for the branded boosters.
- No Reddit r/SMMPanels or BlackHatWorld threads were surfaced by search, so market-insider volume and supply commentary is missing.
